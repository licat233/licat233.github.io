**Product SEO Naming in Practice · V2.0**

A product name may look like nothing more than a title, but it often has to do several different jobs: help buyers find a product on a marketplace, help website visitors understand what is being offered, help Google interpret a page, and help product data match shopping searches.

The problem is that these are not the same field, and they do not all follow the same rules.

For example, Made-in-China.com (MIC) has its own recommendations for product names. An independent website has an H1, an HTML Title, and a Meta Description. If you also submit product data to Google Merchant Center, there is another field: the product Feed Title.

Applying one formula to all of these fields can cause two problems: you may fail to follow a marketplace's requirements, while unnecessarily restricting the titles on your own website.

This guide uses publicly available information from Made-in-China.com, Google Search Central, and Google Merchant Center. It separates official requirements from editorial recommendations and practical examples. A rule from one marketplace should not be presented as a ranking rule for every search engine, and an editorial guideline should not be described as a mandatory Google requirement.

## 1. First, Identify the Product Name Field: These Are Different Fields

| Field | Where it appears | Its main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| MIC Product Name | Product publishing page on Made-in-China.com | Help marketplace buyers identify and find the product |
| Website H1 | The visible main heading on a product detail page | Help visitors quickly understand what the page is about |
| SEO Title (HTML `title`) | Page code and one source for the title shown in search results | Summarize the page and help searchers decide whether to visit |
| Meta Description | The page's description meta tag | Provide a candidate summary for search engines |
| Google Merchant Center Title | Product data Feed | Help Google Shopping and other product placements identify the item accurately |

These fields can use similar product names, but they should not automatically be identical.

For example, MIC's Product Name field has its own recommendations about punctuation and word count. A website's SEO Title can use separators to include a brand name. Google Merchant Center's product title, meanwhile, has a specific maximum length of 150 characters.

**Identify the field you are filling in before applying its rules.** This is one of the easiest details to overlook in product naming, but it is also one of the most important.

## 2. What Does the Official Made-in-China.com Guide Actually Say?

The Made-in-China.com Help Center article (available in Chinese), [How to Set a Product Name](https://service.made-in-china.com/help/promotionskill/product/3243.htm), provides specific recommendations for naming products.

The page states that:

- A product name can be structured as "modifier + noun + for + application + with + certification, patent, etc. + model, standard, feature, etc."
- It recommends a product name of **5–12 words**.
- It advises against using punctuation in the product name.
- Modifiers can be rearranged. Accuracy matters more than word order, and unrelated words should not be added just to match more searches.
- MIC explains that its on-site search can recognize meaning. Repeating similar product names does not produce a corresponding ranking benefit and can make the name harder for buyers to read.

There is one detail about the recommended length that needs to be made clear:

**The MIC Help Center page that can be checked directly specifies 5–12 words.** Some additional platform-operations guidance also uses **8–12 words** as a more focused working target. In practice, you can use 8–12 words as the preferred range while retaining the 5–12-word range stated on the Help Center page.

This does not mean you must add meaningless words just to reach eight. If a product's accurate name is already complete in six or seven words, do not make it less natural or less accurate just to satisfy a number.

Also note that MIC's advice against punctuation applies to the Product Name field on MIC. It should not be copied directly into website SEO Titles, product Feeds, or fields on other platforms.

## 3. What Information Should Go into a Complete Product Name?

The information mentioned in MIC's guide can be grouped into six categories. These are possible sources of useful information, not six mandatory slots that every product title must fill.

| Information type | What question does it answer? | Common examples | Must it be included? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Name | What exactly is this product? | LED Lens Strip, Injection Molding Machine | The product category must be clear |
| Attributes | What important properties does this product have? | White, Flexible, Stainless Steel, 24V | Include only useful attributes |
| Application | Where is the product mainly used? | for Lightboxes, for Food Processing | Prioritize it when the application is clear |
| Certification / Patent | What verifiable credentials or intellectual property apply? | CE, RoHS, patent name or number | Include only when supported and applicable |
| Model / Standard | How can buyers identify the model or applicable standard? | Model number, industry standard, specification grade | Include when it helps buyers search or identify the product |
| Key Features | What function or construction detail should buyers notice? | Wide Beam Optics, Magnetic Mounting | Select the most important features |

### 3.1 Product Name: State the Product Category Clearly

The product name is the core of the title. After reading it, a buyer should at least be able to tell what type of product is being offered.

For example:

- LED Lens Strip
- Injection Molding Machine
- Magnetic Cable Clip
- Stainless Steel Mixing Tank

Do not put several different product categories into one title just to cover more search terms.

If the product is an LED Lens Strip, do not stack LED Strip, LED Light Bar, and LED Lighting together as if they were interchangeable names for the same item. That does not make the product category clearer.

### 3.2 Attributes: Include Only Information That Helps Identify the Product

Attributes can describe color, material, size, construction, power supply, performance, or compatibility.

For example:

- White: color.
- Flexible: construction.
- Stainless Steel: material.
- Wireless: operating characteristic.
- 24V: rated voltage.

Before adding an attribute, ask: if this word were removed, would it become harder for a buyer to tell whether this is the product they need?

If the answer is no, that attribute may not deserve space in the title. Detailed specifications such as dimensions, voltage, weight, and color temperature do not all need to be squeezed into the product name.

### 3.3 Application: Use Terms Buyers Understand

An application should come from the product's actual use, not from adding industry names at random to reach more searches.

For example:

- for Lightboxes
- for Retail Shelving
- for Food Processing
- for Display Cabinets

If a product can be used in several industries, prioritize the main application with the greatest commercial relevance. Other applications can be explained in the product summary, page content, and FAQ.

If the product does not have a clearly defined application, there is no need to force a "for" phrase into the title.

### 3.4 Certifications and Patents: Be Ready to Provide Evidence

Certifications and patents are not decorative keywords.

Only include them when certificates, test reports, patent documents, or other valid evidence support the claim, and the scope of that evidence actually covers the product being named.

For example, a company's management-system certification does not mean that every product it sells has that product certification. A test report, a declaration of conformity, and a formal certificate are also not interchangeable.

If there is no verifiable certification or patent information, do not add it just to make the title look more substantial.

### 3.5 Models and Standards: Consider Whether Buyers Search for Them

A model number or standard can be an important way for buyers to identify a product.

If customers often search by model number when looking for replacement parts, accessories, or a specific specification, the model may deserve a place in the title.

If the model is only an internal inventory code that buyers are unlikely to search for, it can remain in the specification table, product summary, or body content.

The same applies to standards: use a standard's name or number only when the product genuinely conforms to it and you have evidence to support that claim.

## 4. How Do You Find the Right Core Keyword for a Product?

Do not choose a core keyword simply because AI says a particular English name sounds more professional. Whether a term is suitable should be judged from two angles: the facts about the product and the way buyers search.

### Step 1: Confirm the Actual Product Category from Product Information

Start by reviewing product photos, specification sheets, manuals, brochures, and application information. Confirm:

- What are the product's actual structure and functions?
- How is it different from similar products?
- What is it commonly called in the industry?
- Which specifications and features are supported by the available information?

The purpose of this step is to avoid assigning a product to the wrong category just because another keyword has a higher search volume.

### Step 2: Collect the Words Buyers Actually Use

Useful sources include:

- Product names used in customer inquiries, purchasing requests, and emails.
- Terms that salespeople repeatedly hear in conversations with customers.
- Search suggestions, product categories, and buyer search data on e-commerce and B2B platforms.
- Names used on competitors' product pages.
- Common industry terms found in directories and professional materials in the target market.

Keep customers' original wording whenever possible. Buyers do not always use the terminology that engineers prefer. They may use a simpler or more common product name. Good naming requires understanding that difference, not automatically choosing the most technical-sounding term.

### Step 3: Check Whether the Search Results Really Match the Product

Search each candidate keyword on Google or the target marketplace and check:

1. Do the products in the results belong to the same category as yours?
2. Are searchers looking for the product itself, or for a tutorial, spare part, repair service, or something else?
3. Which product names and attribute terms do the leading pages use?
4. Are those terms appropriate for the target market and the language used by its buyers?

If a keyword has a high search volume but its results mainly refer to a different kind of product, do not choose it just because the number looks attractive.

### Step 4: Use Search Console to Validate Existing Search Performance

For a website that is already operating, review the Performance report in Google Search Console.

Google explains that the report can show clicks, impressions, click-through rate, and average position by search query and page. This helps you understand the terms people actually use to reach the site and whether a product page has already gained exposure for relevant searches.

Reference: [Google Search Console: Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en).

Remember that Search Console reports the search performance the website has already received. It is not a complete database of market search volume. If a query does not appear, that does not prove nobody searches for it. Search-volume and difficulty figures from other tools should also be treated as estimates, not as proof that a keyword is relevant to the product.

The research should ultimately identify:

- One accurate core product keyword.
- Several related keywords that can be used naturally in the page content.
- The main application.
- Attributes and differentiating features supported by product evidence.

Do not make the product title responsible for covering every keyword that belongs on the entire detail page.

## 5. How Should You Arrange the Words and Control the Name Length?

### 5.1 A Recommended Structure, Not a Sentence You Must Copy

You can start with the structure suggested by MIC:

`[Modifier] + [Core Product Name] + for [Application] + with [Certification / Patent / Key Feature] + [Model / Standard]`

For example:

**White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes with Wide Beam Optics**

However, this formula does not mean every product must include a modifier, application, certification, patent, model, and standard.

When organizing the final name, prioritize:

1. Whether the product category is clear.
2. Whether the most important attribute appears where buyers can see it easily.
3. Whether the application is accurate and useful.
4. Whether a certification, patent, model, or standard deserves space in the title.
5. Whether the complete name sounds natural.

### 5.2 Use 8–12 Words as the Preferred Working Target

For English product names, 8–12 words can be used as a preferred editorial target.

The MIC Help Center page specifies a public recommendation of 5–12 words, so do not incorrectly describe eight words as the mandatory minimum on that page.

In practice:

- **8–12 words:** Usually a useful target.
- **5–7 words:** If the product category, important attributes, and application are already clear, do not add words just to make the title longer.
- **More than 12 words:** Check whether the title contains too many specifications, repeated terms, secondary applications, or marketing language.

Word count is a tool for keeping a name manageable. It is not a ranking guarantee and cannot replace semantic judgment.

### 5.3 Do Not Apply MIC's Punctuation Advice to Other Fields

MIC's official Help Center advises against punctuation in product names. For the MIC Product Name field, follow that recommendation.

However, a website SEO Title can use `|`, `-`, or `:` to separate a brand from product information. Google Merchant Center product Feed Titles have their own formatting requirements as well.

A field-specific rule should not become a universal restriction for every website and platform.

## 6. Official Examples: What Does Poor Product Naming Look Like?

The MIC guide provides two typical counterexamples.

### Example 1: Stacking Synonymous Product Names

Not recommended:

**Injection Machine / Injection Molding Machine / Injection Moulding Machine**

The problem is that the same title lists several closely related names. Buyers may find it difficult to tell what the product actually is. MIC explains that its on-site search recognizes meaning, so stacking these names does not create an additional ranking advantage.

A better approach is to choose one accurate product name that is commonly used in the target market:

**Injection Molding Machine**

If the product information also confirms that the machine uses a hydraulic system and is mainly used to produce thermoplastic components, the name could be expanded to:

**Hydraulic Injection Molding Machine for Thermoplastic Component Production**

This name contains eight English words when counted by spaces. "Hydraulic" and the application phrase are illustrative: use them only if the product really has a hydraulic structure and the stated application is accurate.

There is also a regional spelling difference between "Injection Molding" and "Injection Moulding." Choose the spelling that best matches the target market and its buyers. Do not put both spellings into the same title.

### Example 2: A Name That Is Too General and Lacks a Useful Attribute

MIC uses **Garlics** as an example of an overly simple name, then shows **Organic Garlic** as a way to add an attribute.

The lesson is not that "longer titles are always better." It is that adding "Organic" can make the product description more specific when the product genuinely has that attribute.

But "Organic" must not be used casually. Include it only when the product and supporting information justify the claim.

Both examples point to the same principle: **A title should not be reduced to an overly broad noun, but it should not be filled with every synonym either. Add information that buyers care about and that you can actually verify.**

## 7. A Complete Naming Walkthrough: From Product Information to the Final Title

The following is an anonymized example using an LED Lens Strip. It retains the product category and relevant technical information without identifying a company brand.

### 7.1 Start by Organizing the Product Facts

| Item | Confirmed information |
|---|---|
| Product category | LED Lens Strip |
| Color | White |
| Main application | Lightboxes |
| Operating voltage | DC 24V |
| Optical characteristic | Approximately 160° beam angle |
| Cutting method | One cuttable section for every 3 LEDs |
| Certification or patent | No evidence for a certification or patent is provided in this example |
| Model | The model is not the primary search entry point in this example |

From these facts, we can identify LED Lens Strip as the core product name and Lightboxes as the main application. White is a useful identifying attribute, while Wide Beam Optics can describe the optical characteristic.

DC 24V and the cutting method are useful specifications, but whether they belong in the name depends on the field's length and what buyers care about most. No certification or patent information has been provided, so neither should be added.

### 7.2 Create the MIC Product Name and Website H1

**White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes with Wide Beam Optics**

This name contains 10 English words. It meets the preferred 8–12-word target and also falls within the 5–12-word range stated on the MIC Help Center page.

Its parts are:

- White: an important attribute.
- LED Lens Strip: the core product name.
- for Lightboxes: the main application.
- with Wide Beam Optics: a differentiating feature.

It does not include DC 24V, the cutting method, a model number, or a certification because those are not the information that needs to be prioritized in this particular name.

### 7.3 Create the Website SEO Title

**White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes | [Brand Name]**

The `|` separator is an editorial choice for a website HTML Title. It is not the format for an MIC Product Name.

Google does not specify that an HTML Title must contain 50–60 characters. A title should be accurate, concise, and distinctive. If it is too long, Google may truncate it according to the available display width. Whether to include the brand depends on whether it helps identify the page; there is no need to mechanically repeat a long brand slogan on every page.

### 7.4 Write the Meta Description

**A 24V LED lens strip for lightboxes with an approximately 160° beam angle and cuttable sections in groups of three LEDs.**

This description adds technical information that is not fully explained in the product name, giving buyers more context in search results.

There is no need to add empty marketing language just to reach a fixed character count. Google may use the Meta Description, or it may select a relevant passage from the page content based on the user's query.

### 7.5 If You Also Submit Products to Google Merchant Center

A product Feed Title can include more attributes that help buyers distinguish the item, for example:

**White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes, 24V, Approx. 160° Beam Angle, 3-LED Cuttable Sections**

Google Merchant Center limits the product title field to 150 characters. Its official guidance requires the title to describe the product on the landing page accurately and to distinguish variants such as different colors or sizes. Important information should appear near the beginning.

This is a different field from the HTML Title used in Google organic search. Do not mistake the 150-character Feed limit for a length rule for website SEO Titles.

Reference: [Google Merchant Center: Title and Structured Title](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324415?hl=en).

## 8. The Different Roles of H1, SEO Title, Meta Description, and Product Feed Title

These four fields are easy to confuse, so each should be handled separately.

### 8.1 H1: Tell Visitors What the Page Is About

The H1 is the visible main heading on a product detail page.

It should:

- Identify the product category accurately.
- Use a product name buyers can understand.
- Add an important attribute or application when useful.
- Match the product actually shown on the page.
- Avoid turning a long list of keywords into the H1.

When generating title links in search results, Google may consider the HTML Title, the main page title, H1 headings, other prominent text, link text, and several other sources. The main heading and HTML Title should therefore describe the page consistently rather than contradict each other.

This does not mean the H1 and HTML Title must be identical. Nor does Google specify a mandatory word count for an H1.

Reference: [Google Search Central: Influence your title links in search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).

### 8.2 SEO Title: Accurate, Concise, and Distinctive

The HTML Title is an important source for the title link Google generates in search results, but Google may combine several sources when deciding what to display.

Google's official guidance makes the following points:

- Every page should have a clear HTML Title.
- The title should accurately and concisely describe the page.
- Do not repeatedly stack keywords.
- Do not use nearly identical template titles for every product page.
- There is no fixed character limit for an HTML Title. Search results may truncate it according to the available display width.

Therefore, do not present "50–60 characters" as a mandatory Google rule. A website may use it as an initial internal editing guideline, but the final check should focus on whether the title is complete, whether it distinguishes the product, and how it appears in actual search results.

### 8.3 Meta Description: Provide a Useful Summary, Not a Keyword List

Google explains that search snippets are primarily generated from page content. Google may also use the Meta Description when it provides a more accurate description of the page.

For that reason:

- Give important product pages their own accurate Meta Descriptions.
- Use them to add useful details such as models, specifications, materials, applications, and key functions.
- Do not simply copy the H1.
- Do not connect a list of keywords with commas.
- Do not present 140–160 characters as a mandatory Google limit.

Google does not specify a fixed character limit for Meta Descriptions. Search snippets may be shortened according to display width, and Google may show different snippets for different queries.

Reference: [Google Search Central: Control your snippets in search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet).

### 8.4 Google Merchant Center Title: Follow the Product Data Requirements

If you submit product data through Google Merchant Center, follow the product Feed specifications.

The official documentation states that:

- The Title field must contain 1–150 characters.
- The title must accurately describe the product on its landing page.
- Variants such as different colors and sizes should include information that distinguishes them.
- The title should use professional, grammatically correct language.
- Do not add information such as price, promotions, or shipping that does not belong in the product name.
- Put important product information near the beginning.

Google Merchant Center also has requirements for AI-generated product titles, including the `structured_title` attribute and the `digital_source_type` designation. When using a product Feed, follow the current official documentation. Do not substitute the rules for a webpage H1 or HTML Title for the Feed requirements.

## 9. Use Python to Check Word Count, Character Count, and Punctuation

AI can miscount words and characters. Whenever an exact count needs to be reported, use a program to calculate it instead of estimating by eye.

The following script checks MIC Product Names, website H1s, SEO Titles, Meta Descriptions, and Merchant Center Feed Titles.

```python
import re
import string
from collections import Counter

fields = {
    "MIC Product Name": (
        "White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes "
        "with Wide Beam Optics"
    ),
    "Website H1": (
        "White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes "
        "with Wide Beam Optics"
    ),
    "SEO Title": (
        "White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes | [Brand Name]"
    ),
    "Meta Description": (
        "A 24V LED lens strip for lightboxes with an "
        "approximately 160° beam angle and cuttable "
        "sections in groups of three LEDs."
    ),
    "Merchant Center Title": (
        "White LED Lens Strip for Lightboxes, 24V, "
        "Approx. 160° Beam Angle, 3-LED Cuttable Sections"
    ),
}

# Check ASCII punctuation in the MIC product name
mic_punctuation = set(string.punctuation)

for name, value in fields.items():
    value = value.strip()

    # Count English words separated by spaces.
    # A hyphenated expression such as 3-LED counts as one word.
    tokens = value.split()

    # Normalize the text before checking for exact repeated words.
    words = re.findall(
        r"[A-Za-z0-9]+(?:[-'][A-Za-z0-9]+)*",
        value.lower()
    )

    counts = Counter(words)
    duplicates = [
        word for word, count in counts.items()
        if count > 1
    ]

    print(f"\n{name}")
    print(f"Characters: {len(value)}")
    print(f"Words: {len(tokens)}")
    print(
        "Repeated words:",
        ", ".join(duplicates) if duplicates else "None"
    )

    if name == "MIC Product Name":
        punctuation = sorted(set(value) & mic_punctuation)
        print(
            "MIC punctuation:",
            ", ".join(punctuation) if punctuation else "None"
        )
```

This script checks:

1. The actual character count, including spaces and punctuation.
2. The English word count based on space-separated tokens.
3. Exact repeated English words.
4. ASCII punctuation used in the MIC Product Name.

A few details are worth noting:

- Hyphenated expressions are counted as one space-separated token in this script. For example, 3-LED counts as one word.
- Different platforms may implement word counting differently. Check the actual count in the platform's publishing interface as well.
- Repeated-word detection is only an auxiliary check. Some product names legitimately repeat a word, so a program finding a repetition does not automatically mean the title is keyword-stuffed.
- The program cannot determine whether a product really has a certification, complies with a standard, or matches a particular search intent.
- For non-English titles, use a tokenization method suitable for the target language instead of applying English word-count rules directly.

The program calculates. AI helps interpret and organize the wording. Product documentation supports the facts. None of these can replace the others.

## 10. A Practical Workflow: From Product Information to Publication

Use the following process whenever you create an SEO name for a product.

### Step 1: Organize the Product Facts

Review product photos, specification sheets, manuals, brochures, and other reliable materials.

Confirm the product category, attributes, applications, model, standards, certifications, and technical features. Do not write information into the title as a product fact unless the available materials support it.

### Step 2: Research the Words Buyers Use

Collect industry terminology, customers' original wording, marketplace search suggestions, search results, and Search Console data.

For each candidate keyword, check whether the product it describes actually matches the product you are naming.

### Step 3: Choose the Core Product Name

Select one product name that is accurate and matches the search habits of buyers in the target market.

Do not put multiple synonyms, regional spellings, or similar product categories into the same title.

### Step 4: Select Important Attributes and Applications

Choose the most useful identifying attributes from the product information and determine the main application.

Not every specification belongs in the title. Prioritize information that helps buyers identify the product quickly.

### Step 5: Decide Whether Certifications, Patents, Models, and Standards Belong in the Name

Check the evidence and search value of each item.

- Include a certification only when it is valid and its scope covers the product.
- Include a patent only when its ownership and scope can be described accurately.
- Include a model when it has search or identification value for buyers.
- Include a standard only when there is clear evidence that it applies to the product.

### Step 6: Create Names for the Relevant Platform Fields

Create the MIC Product Name, website H1, HTML Title, Meta Description, and product Feed Title separately.

Do not force different fields into one identical title.

### Step 7: Validate with a Program

Use Python to check character count, word count, and repeated words. For MIC names, check punctuation as well, then confirm the actual count in the marketplace publishing interface.

### Step 8: Review the Result and Record the Evidence

Check whether the English reads naturally, whether the product facts are accurate, and whether the search results match the needs of the intended buyers.

Keep the reasons behind the keyword choice and the sources for the product facts. This makes future revisions easier to understand instead of leaving behind a title whose logic nobody can explain.

## 11. Final Review Checklist

Before delivering a product name, check at least the following:

- [ ] The product category is accurate and does not misrepresent the item.
- [ ] The core product keyword is clear, without stacking synonymous product names.
- [ ] Attributes come from real product information and help buyers identify the item.
- [ ] The application matches the product's actual use.
- [ ] Certifications, patents, and standards have supporting evidence and apply to the product.
- [ ] The decision to include or omit the model considers buyers' search and identification needs.
- [ ] The MIC Product Name preferably uses 8–12 words while also respecting the 5–12-word recommendation on its Help Center page.
- [ ] The MIC Product Name follows the platform's advice against punctuation.
- [ ] Website H1s, HTML Titles, and Meta Descriptions do not inherit MIC-specific restrictions.
- [ ] Google Merchant Center Feed Titles follow the relevant product data specifications.
- [ ] Character and word counts have been checked by an actual program.
- [ ] The title reads like a normal product name, not a list of keywords.

## Final Thought: Do Not Turn a Product Name into a Keyword List

A product name should first of all be accurate, natural, and understandable to buyers.

Search data helps us understand how buyers search. Platform requirements help us avoid field-specific mistakes. Product documentation supports every factual claim in the name.

But no formula can replace understanding the product itself.

**A good product name is not one that contains every keyword you can think of. It is one that selects the information buyers need, the product genuinely supports, and the title can communicate clearly.**
