## 1. The fundamentals of overseas social media marketing

Overseas social media marketing is not simply posting a few product photos every day. It is not a matter of translating Chinese posts into English and publishing them overseas, either.

At its core, effective overseas social media marketing means consistently publishing content that potential customers want to see, can understand, and can use when making purchasing decisions. The aim is to help the right people discover the company, understand its products, assess whether the supplier is reliable, and eventually send an inquiry, request a sample, or ask for a quotation when a real project comes up. Platform recommendation systems affect who may get the chance to see the content. The customer's purchasing process affects whether they decide to make contact after seeing it. Both matter.

Social media marketing is not a single content task. It is an ongoing marketing activity. In this guide, B2B means business-to-business: one company selling to another company. B2C means business-to-consumer: a company selling directly to consumers. A sales inquiry, or lead, is a question from a potential customer about pricing, technical details, samples, or cooperation. CTA stands for Call to Action. It tells readers what they can do next, such as view a specification sheet, send a drawing, or ask the sales team for a quotation.

A complete social media operation involves:

1. Choosing platforms
2. Defining account positioning
3. Planning content themes and recurring formats
4. Producing photos, graphics, and video assets
5. Editing videos
6. Writing English copy
7. Setting a publishing schedule
8. Using relevant hashtags and keywords
9. Responding to comments
10. Handling direct messages
11. Amplifying content with advertising
12. Reviewing performance data
13. Converting interest into inquiries
14. Building long-term brand assets

Many exporters see little return from social media not because their products are poor, but because they treat social media as a place to upload pages from a product catalogue.

Common mistakes include:

* Posting only isolated product photos on a plain background
* Posting specifications without explaining their practical value
* Uploading casual factory footage without context
* Repeating claims such as "high quality", "best price", or "professional manufacturer"
* Showing no real application
* Addressing none of the customer's problems
* Providing no clear CTA
* Publishing essentially the same content on every platform
* Never reviewing performance
* Having no process for handling direct messages
* Failing to connect social media with the website, email, and sales team

The real value of social media is not how many views one post receives. It is the long-term process of building:

> Reach → Awareness → Trust → Engagement → Inquiry → Sample → Quotation → Cooperation

### Organic reach and paid reach: how they work together

Organic reach means people see a company's content through recommendations, follows, search, shares, or other unpaid distribution. It does not mean the work is free. Filming, editing, maintaining accounts, and replying to customers all take time. Its advantage is that useful content can keep working over time. For example, a clear installation tutorial may still be found through search months after it was published.

Paid reach means using an advertising system to pay for opportunities to generate impressions, clicks, or other selected actions. A company can choose markets, budgets, and the action it wants people to take, allowing content to reach a target audience faster. However, buying ads does not automatically produce qualified inquiries. When advertising stops, the exposure purchased with the budget will usually decline, although the content, audience relationships, and leads already generated can still be useful.

Organic and paid activity are not alternatives that exclude each other. Imagine an industrial pump manufacturer producing four videos: installation, troubleshooting, factory testing, and real applications. Publishing them organically can reveal which topics attract customer interest. Advertising can then help suitable content reach more potential customers in a target market. A company with mature product pages and a capable sales process can also test lead-generation ads directly. It does not have to wait for an organic video to become popular first.

### How social media, the company website, and sales should work together

Social media helps potential customers discover a company and become interested. The website answers more detailed questions about specifications, installation conditions, customization options, company qualifications, and real projects. The sales team learns about the project, recommends a solution, prepares a quotation, and follows up. Not every customer has to visit the website first. Some will send a direct message or leave their details through an advertising form.

For example, an engineer watches a shelf-lighting installation video and wants to confirm the voltage and dimensions. The post should link to the relevant product or solution page, rather than sending the engineer to the homepage to search for the information. When sales receives the inquiry, it is helpful to know which post or campaign brought the customer in. That makes it easier to continue the conversation around the interest the customer has already shown.

## 2. The key differences between B2B and B2C social media

### 1. The goals are different

B2C social media often focuses on:

* Brand awareness
* Product discovery and recommendations
* Adding products to a cart
* Direct purchases
* Repeat purchases
* Follower growth

B2B social media usually focuses on:

* Being discovered by target customers
* Building professional trust
* Getting onto a customer's shortlist of potential suppliers
* Generating inquiries
* Receiving drawings or project requirements
* Encouraging sample testing
* Moving projects toward quotation
* Supporting long-term business relationships

B2B social media should not be judged only by whether an account looks busy. It also needs to demonstrate that the company can deliver. For example, a video showing industrial machinery in operation may attract attention. Before purchasing, however, a customer will usually need to confirm specifications, operating conditions, installation and maintenance requirements, after-sales support, and delivery capability. The company therefore needs both content that attracts attention and technical content that answers these questions. The two types of content do different jobs, and both are necessary.

### 2. The decision cycle is different

A B2C purchase decision may take a few minutes, a few hours, or a few days.

A B2B purchasing decision may take weeks, months, or even more than a year.

When a purchase requires technical evaluation, sample testing, or approval from several people, a customer is unlikely to place an order immediately after watching one video. But the video may introduce the supplier. When a project begins later, the customer may contact that supplier first. Standard components and repeat orders can, of course, move much faster.

That is why long-term visibility matters in B2B social media. Customers should gradually learn:

* What the company makes
* That its products are real and available
* That customization is possible
* That the company has production capabilities
* That it understands the applications in the customer's industry
* That it is more than a temporary trading operation

### 3. The content priorities are different

B2C content often emphasizes:

* Emotion
* Appearance
* Lifestyle
* Promotions
* Personal experience
* Product recommendations

B2B content often emphasizes:

* Solving problems
* Real applications
* Technical details
* Manufacturing capability
* Project experience
* Evidence and data
* Delivery capability

That does not mean B2B content should be dull.

Good B2B content should combine:

> Professional knowledge + Visual clarity + Real-world context + Trust + A clear next step

### 4. The people watching have different roles

B2B content is not necessarily aimed at one person. It often needs to serve several people involved in the same purchasing decision.

| Role | What they care about | Content that can address it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchasing manager | Price, lead time, consistency, supply capability | Factory, production capacity, packaging, shipments, certifications |
| Engineer | Voltage, installation, compatibility, structure | Installation methods, structural details, wiring |
| Designer | Appearance, lighting effect, neatness, visual quality | Application scenes, before-and-after comparisons, close-up images |
| Business owner | Cost, margin, risk, long-term value | Project examples, efficiency improvements, reliable delivery |
| Project manager | Communication cost, installation efficiency, after-sales risk | Solution capability, drawing support, customization process |
| End customer | Product presentation, store image, user experience | Real retail environments and finished results |

B2B content should therefore address the concerns of different decision-makers, not just describe the product itself.

Consider the same light fixture. A designer wants to know how it looks inside a display cabinet. An engineer wants to know how it is wired and whether it fits the structure. A purchasing manager wants to understand lead time and customization. If the company publishes only one product photo with a wattage label, none of these people gets a complete answer. Application photos, installation tutorials, quality-control footage, and packaging videos can each answer a different set of questions.

## 3. Major overseas platforms and how to use them

Before choosing platforms, think about where your customers are and what kind of content your company can produce consistently. Platform roles are not mutually exclusive. YouTube has both search and recommendation traffic. Instagram can build a visual brand and generate inquiries. The descriptions below indicate the priorities worth considering when planning your work.

| Platform | Main value to consider | Good content starting points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook | Advertising, communities, and remarketing | Applications, project examples, installation comparisons |
| Instagram | Visual presentation of products | Real scenes, details, carousels, Reels |
| YouTube | Active search and in-depth learning | Tutorials, explanations, FAQs |
| TikTok | Product discovery by new audiences | Installation actions, tests, production processes |
| LinkedIn | Professional audiences and business relationships | Industry knowledge, engineering projects |
| X | Industry conversations and technical updates | Opinions, news, technical details |

If your customers are mainly engineers, YouTube tutorials and LinkedIn may be good places to start. If the product's value depends heavily on how it looks in use, Instagram and short-form video are also worth considering. Do not sacrifice content quality just to appear on every platform.

### 1. Facebook

#### 1.1 Platform positioning

Facebook is one of the main platforms exporters can use for advertising, remarketing, and community activity. Meta's advertising tools can also manage eligible ad placements on Instagram.

It may not be the most premium B2B content platform, but it is useful for:

* Driving traffic to an independent website
* Running paid advertising
* Remarketing
* Participating in relevant communities
* Building brand credibility
* Promoting trade shows
* Introducing product ranges

Facebook's value is not limited to organic reach from a Page. Its advertising system, Pixel tracking, and audience remarketing capabilities are also important.

A Pixel is a piece of tracking code installed on a website. Subject to privacy requirements, it can report events such as page visits and successful form submissions to an advertising platform. Facebook Page posts, Groups, and Reels are different content entry points; they are not all distributed in exactly the same way. [Meta's explanation of recommendation systems](https://ai.meta.com/blog/how-ai-powers-experiences-facebook-instagram-system-cards/) describes how these different experiences work. When participating in industry Groups, follow the group rules and contribute useful information instead of posting unsolicited advertisements.

#### 1.2 Content that works well on Facebook

Suitable content includes:

* Product application photos
* Factory production videos
* Customer or project examples
* Before-and-after comparisons
* Product installation demonstrations
* Trade show updates
* Short posts about industry problems
* Conversion-focused ad creative
* Remarketing ad creative

Less suitable content includes:

* Isolated product photos on a plain white background
* Posters filled with text
* Product specifications without an application
* Aggressive "factory direct" sales messages
* Repeatedly publishing the same type of image every day

#### 1.3 Facebook content structures

##### Structure 1: Start with a problem

Suitable products:

* LED shelf lighting
* Power track
* ESL
* LCD shelf displays

Template:

> Problem → Why it matters → Solution → Benefit → CTA

Example:

> Shelf lighting is not only about brightness.
> In many retail fixture projects, the real problem is wiring, installation time, and future maintenance.
> A magnetic shelf lighting system helps fixture suppliers keep shelves cleaner and easier to adjust.
> Send us your shelf structure drawing, and we can suggest a lighting layout.

##### Structure 2: Start with a comparison

Template:

> Traditional solution vs. Better solution

Example:

> Traditional shelf lighting:
> Separate cables. Fixed positions. Longer installation. Difficult maintenance.
>
> Magnetic shelf lighting:
> Faster installation. Cleaner shelves. Flexible positioning. Easier replacement.
>
> For retail fixture suppliers, installation efficiency matters as much as brightness.

##### Structure 3: Start with a project example

Template:

> Customer type → Problem → Supplier solution → Result → CTA

The following is a hypothetical teaching example. When publishing a real post, replace it with a genuine project and results that the customer has approved for public use.

> A cosmetic display supplier needed a cleaner lighting system for adjustable shelves.
> The original wiring was difficult to hide and slowed down installation.
> We provided custom-length magnetic LED shelf lights with concealed wiring.
> The result was a cleaner display structure and faster on-site installation.
> Contact us for shelf lighting solutions.

#### 1.4 How to approach Facebook advertising

When a company is new to advertising and does not yet understand customer preferences, it can start by testing creative and audiences with a small budget. A company that already has a suitable landing page, a clear target customer, and a capable sales team can test a Leads objective directly. The following three layers are a useful way to think about common campaign goals, not a mandatory sequence that every company must follow.

##### Layer 1: Test with a cold audience

Goals:

* Find out which creative attracts interest
* Test markets
* Test industries
* Test selling points

Suitable creative:

* Videos that introduce a customer problem
* Before-and-after comparisons
* Product application scenes
* Real factory footage
* Installation demonstrations

Useful metrics:

* Three-second video view rate
* Cost per video view
* Click-through rate
* Page engagement
* Quality of comments

##### Layer 2: Remarketing

The goal is to reach people who have already shown interest.

For example, a purchasing manager in Germany watches an installation video and visits the company website but does not send an inquiry. If privacy requirements are met and the audience is eligible under the platform's rules, the company may show that person a real project example, factory test, or specification information later. Remarketing should not simply repeat the same advertisement endlessly. It should provide information the customer may still need during the purchasing process.

Potential audiences include:

* People who visited the company website
* People who watched 25%, 50%, or 75% of a video
* People who clicked an ad
* People who interacted with the Page
* People who opened a form but did not submit it

Suitable creative:

* Customer or project examples
* Factory capabilities
* Certifications
* Product details
* Solution consultations or sample requests (whether samples are free depends on the company's actual policy)

##### Layer 3: Convert interest into inquiries

The goal is to encourage high-intent customers to submit a form or send a message.

Suitable CTAs:

* Request a quote
* Send your shelf drawing
* Get product specs
* Ask for a custom solution
* Contact us for samples

For B2B companies, these are usually less suitable as the first CTA:

* Buy now
* Order today
* Limited discount

They sound more like consumer retail promotions.

### 2. Instagram

#### 2.1 Platform positioning

Instagram is particularly good at showing the visual value of a product. People can see photos in the feeds of accounts they follow, discover unfamiliar accounts through Explore or Reels, and use Stories for lightweight updates and trade show moments. Each entry point has its own viewing habits, so not every post needs to use the same format.

For a B2B company, Instagram is not only a way to generate leads. It can also help customers feel that:

* The company has a good eye for design
* Its products look well made
* It has real applications to show
* It is not a low-end manufacturer
* It is suitable for higher-end projects

Instagram is particularly relevant to businesses such as ARMOR, which works with retail lighting, store fixtures, and ESL products. These products are installed in retail environments, where visual appearance is part of the value.

#### 2.2 Content that works well on Instagram

Suitable content includes:

* High-quality product application photos
* Premium retail environments
* Before-and-after lighting comparisons
* Close-up product details
* Reels showing product actions
* Carousel posts explaining useful concepts
* Project photos
* Showroom displays
* Well-shot factory moments

Less suitable content includes:

* Casual footage of a cluttered factory
* Posters crowded with specifications
* Low-resolution images
* Videos with distracting backgrounds
* Too much explanatory text on an image
* Excessive promotional content

#### 2.3 Details for Instagram image content

##### Application scenes

Prioritize scenes such as:

* Cosmetics shelves
* Premium supermarket shelves
* Pharmacy shelves
* Wine displays
* Jewelry cabinets
* Convenience store shelves
* Display fixtures in showrooms

At first glance, the image should help customers understand:

> This product can make my shelves, display cabinets, or store look more premium, cleaner, and more professional.

##### Product close-ups

Useful details to show include:

* The edge of an LED light bar
* Magnetic mounting points
* Electrical contacts on a conductive track
* The frame of an ESL
* The display effect of an LCD shelf display
* How the product fits into a shelf structure

A close-up should not merely show the product. It should show the relationship between the product and its application.

##### Before and after

This type of content can attract attention because viewers can immediately see the change created by the product. Keep the camera position, exposure, and environment consistent when taking the two images. Do not create a misleading result through exaggerated post-processing.

For example:

Left image:

* Dark shelves
* Products that do not stand out
* Visible cables
* A cluttered appearance

Right image:

* Evenly illuminated shelves
* Products that stand out more clearly
* No obvious visible wiring
* A more premium overall appearance

Keep the text short:

> From dark shelves to premium product display.

#### 2.4 An Instagram Carousel structure

A Carousel is a post containing multiple images or panels that users can swipe through. It is useful for explaining a slightly complex problem step by step. Instead of squeezing every detail into one crowded poster, give each panel a clear role. The panels should connect logically; do not remove important explanations just to keep the text short.

A suggested structure:

##### Slide 1: A strong headline

For example:

> Why Shelf Lighting Projects Fail

##### Slide 2: Problem 1

> Too many visible cables

##### Slide 3: Problem 2

> Fixed lighting positions

##### Slide 4: Problem 3

> Difficult maintenance

##### Slide 5: The solution

> Magnetic or conductive track lighting can simplify installation.

##### Slide 6: CTA

> Send us your shelf drawing for layout advice.

#### 2.5 Instagram Reels

Get to the point within the first few seconds so viewers can quickly understand why they should keep watching. The first three seconds are an editing guideline, not a fixed recommendation threshold.

Good opening shots include:

* A dark shelf lighting up in one second
* A light bar attaching to the underside of a shelf
* A track light moving quickly along a conductive track
* Multiple ESLs updating prices
* A transition from visible, messy cables to a clean shelf
* A quick sequence showing a product from components to finished goods

Avoid opening with:

* A logo animation
* The company entrance
* Employee greetings
* A product model number
* A slow-paced opening

### 3. YouTube

#### 3.1 Platform positioning

YouTube is one of the most important long-tail content platforms for overseas B2B marketing.

YouTube has several discovery routes: Search, Home recommendations, Suggested videos, and Shorts. Search is especially valuable when customers are actively researching a technology or product. A clear tutorial can meet that need. Recommendations can also introduce the company to people who were not searching for the product. See [YouTube's official explanation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en).

Customers may actively search for:

* How to install shelf lighting
* What is an electronic shelf label
* Magnetic shelf light installation
* Supermarket shelf lighting solution
* Conductive track lighting for retail shelves
* ESL vs. paper price tags
* How digital shelf labels work

YouTube videos should therefore be built around customer questions, not just around a company introduction.

#### 3.2 Content that works well on YouTube

Suitable content includes:

* Product installation tutorials
* Explanations of how a system works
* Factory tours
* Customer or project examples
* Product comparisons
* Industry knowledge
* FAQ videos
* Application explanations
* Full trade show introductions

Less suitable content includes:

* Short clips with no clear title or purpose
* Videos with music but no useful information
* Product rotation footage with no explanation
* Titles that consist only of an internal model number
* Videos with no relevant search terms or clear topic

#### 3.3 How to write YouTube titles

A weak title:

> ARMOR SLIM-213BWR Product Video

Problems:

* It contains no useful search phrase
* Viewers may not know what the product is
* It does not explain why the video is worth watching

A clearer title:

> 2.13 Inch Electronic Shelf Label for Supermarket Price Display

A more benefit-focused title:

> How 2.13 Inch Electronic Shelf Labels Help Supermarkets Update Prices Faster

A useful title pattern is:

> How / What / Why + Product Keyword + Application + Customer Benefit

The title should accurately describe the video. Use keywords naturally in the title, description, captions, or chapters; there is no need to repeat them excessively. Choose a thumbnail that shows the result. Include links to relevant product pages and contact information in the description. For longer tutorials, add chapters so engineers can find the steps they need.

Examples:

* How to Install Magnetic LED Shelf Lights for Retail Fixtures
* What Is an Electronic Shelf Label and How Does It Work?
* Conductive Track Lighting for Adjustable Retail Shelves
* ESL vs. Paper Price Tags: What Retailers Should Know
* How Shelf Lighting Improves Product Visibility in Stores

#### 3.4 YouTube video structures

##### Installation tutorial

1. Show the finished result first
2. Explain the application
3. Show the required components
4. Demonstrate the installation steps
5. Explain important details and precautions
6. Show the finished lighting effect
7. Explain available customization options
8. Add a CTA

##### Product explainer

1. What is the product?
2. Where is it used?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. How is it different from a traditional solution?
5. What are its key specifications?
6. What customization options are available?
7. Which customers or projects is it suitable for?
8. How can the viewer get in touch?

##### Factory video

1. Company exterior or entrance
2. Raw material area
3. Production line
4. Assembly process
5. Testing
6. Aging tests
7. Packaging
8. Shipment
9. Showroom or application displays
10. CTA

The most important purpose of a factory video is not simply to prove that the company has a factory. It is to answer the questions customers are likely to have:

> Are you a real manufacturer? Are you professional? Can you produce consistently? Can you deliver?

### 4. TikTok

#### 4.1 Platform positioning

TikTok is known for personalized interest-based recommendations, and it also has a search function. B2B companies can use TikTok, but they should not simply upload traditional corporate promotional videos.

#### 4.2 TikTok content ideas for B2B

Suitable content includes:

* Strong physical actions
* Clear visual contrasts
* Factory processes
* Product tests
* Quick installation
* Before-and-after comparisons
* Surprising questions or demonstrations
* Real production
* Bulk shipments
* Finished results

Less suitable content includes:

* Long explanations of specifications
* Company introductions
* Static posters
* Very slow pacing
* Overly formal corporate films

#### 4.3 A TikTok video structure

For a simple installation action, test a short version of around 10–18 seconds. A complete tutorial or complex test needs more time and should not be shortened artificially. Here is an example script.

##### 0–3 seconds

Start with a hook.

Example:

> No visible wiring at the light, yet it works.

Visual:

A track light attaches to the conductive track and lights up immediately.

##### 3–8 seconds

Show what happens.

Example:

Move the light to another position by hand. It continues to work.

##### 8–14 seconds

Show the result.

Example:

The entire shelf is evenly illuminated, with no obvious visible wiring.

##### 14–18 seconds

Add a CTA.

Example:

> Comment "TRACK" for product specs.

#### 4.4 TikTok hook ideas

##### Contrast

* No visible wires at the light, but it still lights up.
* This shelf looks normal until the light turns on.
* We moved the shelf light in 3 seconds.

##### Pain point

* Shelf lighting should not take hours to install.
* Most shelf lighting problems start with wiring.
* Paper price tags slow down store operations.

##### Result

* Watch this dark shelf turn premium.
* From aluminum profile to finished shelf light.
* One rail powers multiple lights.

##### Question

* How would you light this shelf without visible cables?
* Why do supermarkets replace paper price tags?
* What makes shelf lighting difficult to install?

## 4. How to adapt one product for different platforms

Let's use a conductive track lighting system as an example.

When filming, keep horizontal and vertical footage, the complete installation process, close-ups, and wider shots of the environment. You can then edit the material for different platforms. In this example, "wireless" means that no separate visible connecting cable is needed between the light and the track. The track itself still needs a power connection. Do not mislead viewers into thinking that the system requires no electrical wiring at all.

### 1. Facebook version

Main focus:

* Installation efficiency
* Lower installation costs
* Suitability for conversion-focused ads

Copy direction:

> Traditional shelf lighting requires separate wiring for every light.
> Conductive track lighting allows light modules to connect through the rail, making installation cleaner and faster.
> Suitable for supermarket, pharmacy, and display shelf projects.

### 2. Instagram version

Main focus:

* Premium visual presentation
* The appearance of the shelf
* Brand image

Content formats:

* Premium shelf application photos
* Close-ups of the light attaching to the track
* Before-and-after comparisons
* Carousels explaining how the system works

Headline:

> Less visible wiring. More flexible placement.

### 3. YouTube version

Main focus:

* Explaining the working principle
* Installation tutorials
* Long-tail search

Title:

> How Conductive Track Lighting Works for Adjustable Retail Shelves

Structure:

1. Problems with traditional wiring
2. The structure of the conductive track
3. How the light makes electrical contact
4. How to install the system
5. Which projects it suits
6. CTA

### 4. TikTok version

Main focus:

* Strong physical actions

## 5. Multi-account strategies

### 1. What a multi-account strategy is really for

A multi-account strategy is not about registering as many accounts as possible.

Its purpose is to let different accounts serve different customer groups, rather than copying the same content everywhere. Imagine a company that manufactures both industrial lighting and commercial lighting. Industrial customers may care about protection, reliability, and installation, while commercial customers may care about the appearance of a space and ease of maintenance. Separate accounts are worthwhile only when there is enough distinct content for each audience.

The same applies to regional accounts. It makes sense to separate them when local languages, certification requirements, purchasing habits, or applications are genuinely different. If the company does not have enough material or staff, it should focus on its main account first.

### 2. Types of accounts in a content matrix

#### Main brand account

For example (the names below are illustrative, not a list of existing accounts):

* ARMOR

Purpose:

* The main entry point for the brand
* Overall company image
* Showcase of core product lines
* Building company credibility

#### Product-line accounts

Examples:

* ARMOR Retail Lighting
* ARMOR Power Track
* ARMOR Digital Shelf

Purpose:

* Focus on a vertical customer group
* Make content more relevant
* Create space for specialized content

#### Regional accounts

Examples:

* ARMOR Europe
* ARMOR Middle East
* ARMOR LATAM
* ARMOR Southeast Asia

Purpose:

* Publish localized-language content
* Show local market projects
* Promote local trade shows
* Address local customer habits

#### Platform accounts

The way content is presented should differ by platform:

* LinkedIn: professional topics and industry insights
* Instagram: visuals and applications
* YouTube: tutorials and search
* Facebook: advertising and remarketing
* TikTok: short-form video discovery

### 3. Consistency across region, language, and account identity

Platforms may use signals such as device, language, location, and interactions to understand content relevance. The signals differ by platform, and there is no public evidence that using an IP address from a particular country guarantees local reach. Do not treat the network exit location as a shortcut to regional marketing. Use actual audience data to evaluate results.

The work that really matters is localization: use the language and units customers are familiar with, discuss issues that matter to projects in that market, schedule posts at reasonable local times, and interact with local industry accounts. If you need more direct control over the location of an advertising audience, use the geographic targeting options allowed by the platform. Keep account information genuine, use company-authorized devices and stable account access, and do not pretend to be somewhere you are not. Avoid mass registration, purchased accounts, and simulated engagement.

### 4. A sensible order for launching a multi-account strategy

Do not create too many accounts at the beginning.

A suggested sequence:

#### Stage 1: Establish the basic accounts

## 6. Video marketing for B2B

### 1. The main purpose of B2B video

Video is not about making noise. It is about reducing the effort a customer needs to make when evaluating a supplier.

When watching a video, a customer is effectively asking:

* Is this company real?
* Does this product actually exist?
* Is the product suitable for my project?
* Can the factory deliver?
* Is quality control reliable?
* Is installation complicated?
* Will communication be professional?
* Is this supplier worth contacting?

B2B videos should answer the questions customers have in mind.

One video does not need to answer every question. A 15-second installation demonstration can show how something is installed. A two-minute tutorial can explain which components are needed, how wiring works, and what to do if a problem occurs. A factory testing video can help answer whether the supplier can deliver reliably. These focused videos are more useful than trying to fit every specification into one short clip.

### 2. Three core types of B2B video

#### Product demonstration

Focus on:

* What the product is
* Where it is used
* How it is installed
* How it works
* What problem it solves

Do not film only the product's appearance. Show it in a real application.

#### Product applications

Focus on:

* Whether the scene is real
* Whether it matches the customer's industry
* Whether viewers can imagine using it in their own project
* Whether the change or result is visible

An application scene often provides more context than a product photo on a plain background.

#### Factory environment

Focus on:

* Real production
* Production capacity
* Quality control
* Packaging
* Shipping
* Customization capability
* Delivery reliability

A factory video should not simply show off machinery. It should build trust.

## 7. How short-form video distribution works

### 1. A practical way to understand recommendations

A simplified way to think about it is:

> Content that reaches the right users, provides value, and generates positive viewing or interaction signals is generally in a better position to receive further distribution.

This is an experience-based model to help explain the idea. It is not a mathematical formula published by any platform.

Platforms may consider user behavior such as:

* Whether people stop scrolling
* Whether they watch to the end
* Whether they replay the video
* Whether they like it
* Whether they comment
* Whether they share it
* Whether they save it
* Whether they follow the account
* Whether they visit the profile
* Whether they send a direct message

### 2. How a video may gain more exposure

It is useful to think of some video distribution as "testing with a smaller audience, then potentially expanding recommendations." However, there is no single public system of fixed traffic-pool levels and promotion thresholds shared by all platforms. Content may also be discovered again long after its original publication. Use actual performance data to identify problems instead of assuming an account has been restricted based on rumors.

Useful metrics include:

#### Early retention

Early retention measures whether viewers continue watching after a video starts. For example, looking at the share of viewers still watching at the three-second mark can help identify whether an opening is effective. Platform metric names, starting points, and calculation methods are not identical.

#### Completion rate

Completion rate indicates the proportion of viewers who watch to the end. Do not compare a 15-second video directly with a two-minute tutorial using completion rate alone. A longer tutorial may have a lower completion rate but still generate more meaningful watch time and inquiries.

#### Replay rate

Repeated viewing may mean the content is interesting, or it may mean an action happened too quickly for viewers to understand. Review the retention graph and comments before drawing a conclusion. Some platforms do not provide this metric separately; use the available view and retention data instead.

#### Share rate

Do viewers think the content is useful, interesting, or relatable enough to share?

#### Comment rate

Does the content give viewers a reason to respond?

#### Profile visit rate

Does the content make viewers interested in the account?

#### Direct-message rate

A direct message may be closer to a business opportunity, but it may also be a general question from an ordinary viewer. A company can define its own ratio of messages to impressions, profile visits, or video views. Keep the denominator consistent when comparing results, then check how many messages represent genuine purchasing needs.

### 3. Why the first three seconds matter

The opening seconds influence whether people continue watching. When someone is scrolling through short videos from unfamiliar accounts, showing the product, an action, or a problem immediately is often more effective than starting with a long company introduction. Three seconds is a working guideline, not an algorithmic rule.

Weak openings:

* Logo
* Company name
* Product model number
* Long subtitles
* Slow-motion footage
* Employee greetings
* Empty establishing shots

Stronger openings:

* Turn the product on immediately
* Show the installation immediately
* Start with a comparison
* Ask a direct question
* Show the result first
* Create a clear visual contrast

### 4. Seven content angles that can attract attention

The following seven angles are ideas for developing content, not a checklist for manipulating comments. The headline and visuals must deliver what they promise. Do not deliberately make false claims or stage fake tests or customer projects.

#### 1. Give viewers something they may want to correct

Example:

> Most shelf lighting problems are not caused by LEDs.

Viewers may want to know what actually causes the problem.

#### 2. Let viewers demonstrate what they know

Example:

> Only retail fixture designers will notice this problem.

The target audience may recognize itself in the statement.

#### 3. Make viewers want to see the result

Example:

> Watch this dark shelf turn premium in 2 seconds.

Use a genuine before-and-after comparison.

#### 4. Invite viewers to suggest a solution

Example:

> How would you hide the wires on this shelf?

This can encourage useful discussion.

#### 5. Let viewers see whether a test succeeds or fails

Example:

> We ran an extended test on this shelf light.

Viewers want to see the test result.

#### 6. Let viewers verify whether something is real

Example:

> No visible wiring at the light, yet it works.

Viewers want to understand and verify the working principle.

#### 7. Help viewers recognize their own situation

Example:

> If you design supermarket shelves, this problem is familiar.

This speaks directly to a specific audience.

## 9. A practical framework for evaluating content assets

A product photo on a plain background is useful in a catalogue or specification explanation, but it often lacks application context when the goal is to attract unfamiliar customers. The scoring levels below are mainly for evaluating primary social media assets used for customer acquisition. They do not apply to every type of business document.

### 1. Assets below 60 points in a social media acquisition context

#### Product photos on a plain background

Limitations:

* No application scene
* No sense of scale
* No customer problem
* No installation context
* Little trust-building context
* Limited potential for sharing

They are still suitable for:

* Product catalogues
* Quotations
* Product detail pages
* Specification sheets

Do not rely on this type of image alone when trying to attract new audiences. It remains useful when explaining specifications, showing product appearance, or supporting a purchasing discussion.

#### Screenshots of product catalogues

Limitations:

* Difficult to read on a phone
* Too much text
* Too much information in one image
* Poor fit with common social media viewing habits
* Easily mistaken for an advertisement and skipped

If a catalogue screenshot must be used, redesign the information for a social media format.

### 2. A 60-point asset

#### Casual photos taken at a trade show

Advantages:

* Authentic
* Show activity and atmosphere
* Provide brand updates

Disadvantages:

* Cluttered composition
* Uncontrolled lighting
* No clear focus
* May not communicate product value

Ways to improve them:

* Crop the image
* Remove distracting background details where appropriate
* Add one clear topic
* Include a short explanation
* Avoid publishing the untouched photo without context

#### A product poster with basic copy

Advantages:

* Clearer than a plain product photo
* Can communicate one selling point

Disadvantages:

* Can look templated
* May have limited visual appeal
* Lacks a real application scene

Ways to improve it:

* Reduce the amount of text
* Focus on one main benefit
* Add an application background
* Avoid exaggerated typography and low-end promotional styling

### 3. An 80-point asset

#### Product application photos

Characteristics:

* Customers can see where the product is used
* Customers can imagine it in their own projects
* The result is visible
* The image is more useful for brand building

Examples:

* LED light bars installed on cosmetics shelves
* ESLs installed on supermarket shelf edges
* LCD shelf displays showing promotional content
* Conductive tracks concealed within a shelf structure

#### Real factory footage with a clear selling point

Characteristics:

* Authenticity
* Trust
* Evidence of manufacturing capability

The important question is not "Do we have machines?" It is:

> How does this production process reduce risk for the customer's project?

For example:

> Pre-packing lighting tests help reduce on-site installation issues.

### 4. A 100-point asset

The 60-, 80-, and 100-point levels are illustrative creative ratings, not platform standards. Content designed to attract attention, explain technology, and build trust has different purposes. For a primary social media asset intended to attract customers and encourage further consideration, the following four capabilities can each be scored out of 25:

#### Ability to stop the scroll

Will people pause to watch?

Possible approaches:

* Strong contrast
* Strong physical action
* A clear question
* A visible result
* A noticeable visual change

#### Ability to explain the product

Within five seconds, can viewers understand:

* What the product is
* Where it is used
* What problem it solves

#### Ability to build trust

Can viewers see evidence of:

* A real factory
* A real product
* Real testing
* Real projects
* Real delivery capability

#### Ability to encourage action

After watching, do viewers know what to do next?

* Ask for a quotation
* Request specifications
* Send a drawing
* Request a sample
* View project examples
* Contact the sales team

## 12. Writing for an English-speaking B2B audience

### 1. Do not translate Chinese copy word for word

A common Chinese export-marketing statement is:

> We are a professional manufacturer. Our quality is good, our prices are competitive, and you are welcome to contact us.

A literal translation often becomes:

> We are a professional manufacturer with high quality and competitive price. Welcome to contact us.

This does not differentiate the company.

Almost every supplier can say the same thing.

### 2. English B2B copy should focus more on the customer's problem

An English-language B2B audience may respond more strongly to a message such as:

> Shelf lighting installation is often slowed down by wiring and fixed mounting positions.
> A magnetic shelf lighting system helps fixture suppliers install faster, adjust positions more easily, and keep the shelf structure cleaner.

The difference is not that Chinese people and foreign customers naturally think in completely different ways. It is the difference between supplier-centered copy and customer-problem-focused copy. Chinese writing should also put the reader first.

In English, use clear sentences and words familiar to the customer. Instead of saying "good quality", show the actual tests and acceptance criteria. If all you need to say is that the length can be customized, "Custom lengths available" is enough.

### 3. Replace vague claims with specific value

Use less of:

* high quality
* best price
* professional manufacturer
* leading supplier
* good service
* welcome to contact us

Use more of:

* reduce installation time
* hide visible wiring
* improve product visibility
* support adjustable shelves
* simplify maintenance
* custom length available
* suitable for supermarket fixtures
* tested before shipment
* designed for retail display systems

### 4. Build a company content library instead of searching for material every day

Many teams do not actually lack content. They lack a clear place to find it. Customer project photos may be on salespeople's phones, test videos may be on an engineer's computer, and product photos may be buried in catalogues or chat histories. When the social media manager needs to publish something, they end up using a random product photo just to fill the schedule.

A company can create a shared content library and organize it by product and project. Keep original photos, original video files, approved product information, and finished published assets. For each group of assets, record the product model, application, shooting date, and permission to publish. If a customer name or project location appears, confirm that it can be made public before publishing. The purpose of a shared library is not to create a complicated approval system. It is to help the team find accurate, reusable material quickly.

For example, when filming a light installation, do more than cut a 15-second short video. Keep the full installation footage, horizontal and vertical versions, before-and-after lighting photos, connector close-ups, and test images. Months later, another member of the team can use the same material for a tutorial, an advertisement, a carousel, or a customer FAQ without filming everything again.

Maintain a simple content and publishing log. It can include the asset location, product, topic, publication permission, first publication date, platforms used, results, and ideas for improvement. A shared spreadsheet is enough for a small team. There is no need to build a dedicated management system first.

## 14. Measuring results and reviewing performance

A performance review should help the team decide what to change next time. Start with the goal of the content, then look at the relevant result. For a tutorial, check how far viewers get through the steps. For a lead-generation campaign, check how many genuine project inquiries it produces. Platforms use different definitions for views and clicks, so their numbers should not be added together without checking the measurement methods.

### 1. Metrics to review on each platform

#### TikTok / Reels / Shorts

Focus on:

* Three-second retention
* Completion rate
* Replay rate
* Share rate
* Comment rate
* Follow rate

Do not look only at view count.

#### LinkedIn

Focus on:

* Engagement from target customers
* Quality of comments
* Saves
* Direct messages
* Company Page visits
* Personal profile visits
* Inquiry quality

Imagine an industrial connector manufacturer whose factory machinery video gets 100,000 views but no inquiries. Another installation video gets only 800 views but generates two project inquiries from equipment manufacturers. For a team whose goal is to attract business customers, the second video is more worth testing further. The first may still help increase awareness, but view count alone cannot establish commercial value. These figures are illustrative.

#### Facebook

Focus on:

* Click-through rate
* Cost per engagement
* Quality of comments
* Cost per form submission
* Remarketing conversions
* Website visits

#### Instagram

Focus on:

* Saves
* Shares
* Profile visits
* Direct messages
* Reels completion
* Story clicks

#### YouTube

Focus on:

* Search traffic
* Average view duration
* Click-through rate
* Subscriber conversion
* Website clicks
* Inquiry sources

### 2. Questions for a weekly review

First record the publication date, platform, topic, opening, CTA, link, and result. Compare content with similar goals on the same platform. Where possible, change one major factor at a time.

At least once a week, answer:

1. Which post received the most views, and why?
2. Which post generated the most engagement, and why?
3. Which post led to direct messages or inquiries?
4. Which opening performed best?
5. Which type of visual held attention longest?
6. Which platform suits the current product best?
7. Which posts attracted views but had little customer value?
8. Which posts received modest views but generated good inquiries?
9. Which assets can be edited into new versions?
10. Which content type should receive more attention next week?

### 3. When you see a problem, what should you change?

| What you observe | What to check first | Possible adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many viewers leave at the beginning | Whether the first screen is clear and matches the title | Show the finished result first; remove a long intro |
| Watch time is good, but few people visit the profile | Whether the content is purely entertaining and whether the account's positioning is clear | Add application information and contact details |
| Many clicks, but few website visits or form submissions | Links, mobile loading speed, and whether the landing page matches the ad | Fix the entry point, improve the landing page, simplify the form |
| Many inquiries, but most are not target customers | Region, ad copy, product pricing, and application claims | Make the B2B use case clearer, adjust targeting, add necessary qualification questions |
| Few views, but genuine project inquiries | Audience relevance and acquisition cost | Test similar topics instead of chasing broad reach |

For example, the average watch time of an installation video drops from 12 seconds to 5 seconds. That does not immediately mean people have lost interest in the product. Compare the openings, lengths, audiences, and traffic sources of the two versions. If the new version has a five-second brand intro while the old version shows the installation in the first second, it is worth testing a version without the intro. Change one major factor and then review the result. That is how you learn whether the adjustment worked.

## 15. The basics of paid advertising

### 1. Do not start with a large budget

Advertising budget should first be used to validate commercially valuable results, then expanded gradually. A company with mature content should still confirm its target market, tracking setup, and inquiry quality instead of focusing only on cheap video views.

A common approach is:

1. Test several creative variations with a small budget and choose optimization metrics that match the business goal.
2. Identify creative that attracts target customers and produces valuable actions, not simply the highest click-through rate.
3. If the audience is large enough and privacy requirements are met, build remarketing audiences.
4. Use project examples and factory content to provide the trust signals customers need.
5. Encourage inquiries and continue optimizing with feedback from sales. A mature company can also start directly with an inquiry-generation objective.

### 2. Divide advertising creative by audience temperature

#### Cold-audience creative

Goal:

Attract the attention of people who do not yet know the company.

Suitable content:

* Problem-focused videos
* Comparison videos
* Application images
* Short-form video hooks

#### Warm-audience creative

Goal:

Build trust.

Suitable content:

* Factory videos
* Testing videos
* Customer or project examples
* Project showcases
* Certifications

#### Hot-audience creative

Goal:

Encourage an inquiry.

Suitable content:

* Sample requests (fees depend on the company's actual policy)
* Requests for specification sheets
* Solution consultations
* Drawing submissions
* Meeting bookings
* Quotation requests

### 3. B2B advertising CTAs

Recommended:

* Request a Quote
* Contact Us
* Get Product Specs
* Send Your Project Details
* Ask for a Custom Solution
* Get a Lighting Layout
* Request Samples

Less suitable:

* Buy Now
* Shop Now
* Limited Time Sale

These can be appropriate for clear B2C offers or low-value standardized products, but they are usually not the best first message for a B2B project.

### 4. Prepare before launching: make the campaign measurable

Before clicking "Publish" in an advertising dashboard, make sure customers can contact the company and that the business can identify where inquiries come from. At a minimum, prepare a company Page, a compliant ad account and payment method, genuine product assets, a landing page or instant form that matches the ad, and staff who can reply promptly. The company should retain control of its accounts, assign permissions by role, enable two-factor authentication, and avoid leaving passwords or administrator access with unrelated people.

If you use a website form, you can use the platform Pixel or a tag to record visits and successful submissions. Add UTM parameters to advertising links as well. UTM parameters are labels added to a URL so that an analytics tool can distinguish traffic sources. For example: utm_source=facebook, utm_medium=paid_social, and utm_campaign=power_track_test. Track successful form submissions, not just clicks on the submit button. Meta and other platforms also support server-side conversion events, such as the Conversions API. A small company does not need to delay advertising while building a complex tracking system, but it should test the basic form and conversion tracking before spending money.

An instant form inside a platform lets customers submit information without leaving the app, which can reduce friction. A website form can collect more technical and project qualification details. Both are worth testing. When collecting names, business email addresses, phone numbers, or other personal information, explain how the information will be used, provide an appropriate privacy policy, and comply with the privacy requirements and platform policies that apply to the customer. Do not upload customer lists to an advertising platform if their origin and permission are unclear.

### 5. How should you choose an advertising objective?

The advertising system uses the objective you select to find people who are more likely to take the corresponding action. If the business wants purchasing inquiries, it should not optimize indefinitely for the cheapest video views.

| Current business goal | Common advertising objective | What to monitor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Help more target customers discover the company | Awareness or video views | Reach, meaningful views, distribution across target markets |
| Get customers to read a product or technical page | Website traffic | Landing-page visits, visit quality, subsequent actions |
| Generate purchasing inquiries | Leads or form submissions | Cost per qualified inquiry, company and project fit |
| Encourage conversations about a project | Messages, Leads, or an available conversation objective | Meaningful conversations, response speed, handoff to sales |
| Sell a standardized product directly online | Sales or website conversions | Cost per sale, revenue, gross margin |

The actual names vary by platform, account, and region. For example, Meta's campaign objectives include Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion, and Sales. The available optimization actions depend on the objective and placement. LinkedIn supports professional attribute targeting and on-platform Lead Gen Forms. TikTok also supports lead campaigns using website forms. See the official references at the end of this guide. You do not need to memorize the location of every button in every advertising dashboard.

### 6. How to choose platforms and audiences

The first step in advertising is to identify who you are selling to, not to select a few interest labels at random. Imagine a supplier of shelf lighting for chain stores. Its potential customers may include shelf manufacturers, retail project contractors, and purchasing teams at retail brands. They care about different things. Shelf manufacturers may focus on installation compatibility, while retail brands may care more about store presentation and future maintenance. Creating different creative for these groups is usually more relevant than targeting everyone with one broad interest such as "LED light".

| Platform | Advertising approach |
| --- | --- |
| Facebook / Instagram | Manage ads through Meta. Test product applications, installation actions, project examples, and forms. Region, language, conversion objective, and creative all help shape relevance. |
| LinkedIn | Use relevant industry, function, job title, and company attributes to reach people involved in purchasing decisions. Test professional content downloads and inquiry forms. Very narrow audiences may limit delivery, so check estimated reach. |
| YouTube | Use Google Ads to select suitable video campaigns or other ad formats that support YouTube. Reach audiences with demonstrations, project examples, and technical explanations, supported by conversion tracking. |
| TikTok | Test vertical videos with clear product actions. Use available location, audience, and lead-generation options, then assess whether the resulting leads include enough genuine B2B customers. |
| X | If the target industry is active on the platform, test industry topics, website visits, or available conversion campaigns. Do not advertise there merely to claim platform coverage. |

Audiences can be grouped into cold, warm, and hot stages. Where the account and privacy rules allow, a company can also use properly obtained customer lists, website visitors, or people who have engaged with content to build custom audiences. Lookalike audiences may help find users with similar characteristics, but their actual quality still needs to be tested. A beginner does not need to split a small budget across a dozen ad groups. If each group receives too little data, it becomes difficult to judge the results.

### 7. How to set a budget and test creative

Here is a hypothetical teaching example. A company has USD 300 for a two-week test in one of its main export markets. Its goal is to find potential customers willing to share project requirements. It prepares three pieces of creative: an installation comparison, an application example, and a factory test. It uses the same main advertising objective and keeps the landing page or form broadly consistent. Part of the budget is used for the initial test, while the rest is reserved for the better-performing creative and follow-up adjustments. USD 300 is an example for calculation, not a universal minimum budget and not a guarantee of inquiries.

During testing, distinguish a creative problem from an audience problem. If you change the country, image, copy, budget, and form at the same time, you will not know which change caused the result. At the beginning, keep the market consistent and compare creative. Once you find a promising direction, compare markets or audiences. An advertising platform's automatic budget allocation may not give every creative exactly the same exposure. If you need a fairer comparison, use the platform's supported A/B testing tools.

Do not immediately turn off an ad because it has not generated an inquiry in one day. Do not double the budget just because the click-through rate looks high once. First rule out obvious technical problems such as broken tracking, ad rejection, payment failure, or a landing page that will not load. Then judge the spend, lead quality, and costs the business can afford. You need enough results to make a comparison; the appropriate observation period depends on the budget, audience size, and sales cycle. When increasing the budget, keep watching the quality of new leads rather than following a fixed daily increase.

### 8. Understanding advertising metrics

Advertising dashboards contain many English abbreviations. Beginners do not need to memorize all of them immediately. Start by understanding the path from impressions to clicks, from clicks to inquiries, and from inquiries to sales. That makes it easier to understand where each metric fits.

| Metric | Meaning and calculation | What it helps you understand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions | Number of times the ad was shown; one person can generate multiple impressions | How many times the ad was displayed |
| Reach | Number of unique people reached | How many different people saw the ad |
| Frequency | Impressions ÷ Reach | How often people saw the ad; review alongside performance to detect creative fatigue |
| CPM | Cost per thousand impressions = Spend ÷ Impressions × 1,000 | Cost of buying exposure |
| CTR | Click-through rate = Relevant clicks ÷ Impressions × 100% | Whether the creative encourages clicks; distinguish all clicks from link clicks |
| CPC | Cost per click = Spend ÷ Relevant clicks | How much each click costs |
| CVR | Conversion rate = Conversions ÷ Relevant visits or clicks × 100% | Whether people complete the desired action after clicking; keep the denominator consistent |
| CPL | Cost per lead = Spend ÷ Number of leads | Cost of obtaining one inquiry or form submission |
| CPA | Cost per action = Spend ÷ Number of target actions | Depends on the chosen action, such as a qualified inquiry or sale |
| ROAS | Return on ad spend = Attributed revenue ÷ Ad spend | Revenue attributed to advertising; it is not the same as profit |

For example, suppose a campaign spends USD 200 and receives 20,000 impressions, 400 link clicks, and 20 inquiries. Only five inquiries match the company's target customer profile, and one order is eventually closed with USD 2,000 in attributed revenue. CPM is USD 10, link CTR is 2%, CPC is USD 0.50, click-to-inquiry conversion is 5%, and CPL is USD 10. However, the cost per qualified inquiry is USD 40, the advertising cost per closed order is USD 200, and ROAS is 10. These are simulated figures.

The important distinction is that 20 form submissions do not necessarily mean 20 people with genuine purchasing needs. Another campaign may have a CPL of USD 15 but a much higher qualification and closing rate, making it more profitable. Revenue is not profit either. Product costs, freight, commissions, and other expenses still need to be deducted. For long B2B sales cycles, advertising costs incurred today may not generate revenue for several months. Sales should therefore record the source and progress of each lead, then review quotations, samples, closed orders, and actual gross margin over time.

### 9. When should you stop an ad, and when should you continue?

Start by calculating the cost per qualified inquiry the business can afford. For example, suppose a new order generates USD 1,000 in contribution margin, and historical data shows that one order is closed for every ten qualified inquiries. The average contribution margin associated with one qualified inquiry is approximately USD 100. The company still needs to leave room for other acquisition and sales costs. This does not mean it should spend the full USD 100 on advertising. It means using real business data to set a reasonable limit instead of copying an advertising-cost benchmark from the internet.

If an ad has an obvious technical problem, pause it and fix the problem first. If impressions are high but target customers are not clicking, review the creative and targeting. If clicks are reasonable but qualified inquiries are not coming in, check the landing page and form. If the cost per qualified inquiry is acceptable and sales can follow up, the budget can be increased gradually. If enough data has been collected and the cost is clearly above the business's limit, stopping or adjusting the campaign is more sensible than spending more and hoping for a different result.

Advertising attribution reports may differ from website analytics and CRM records. Different platforms may also claim credit for the same order. Use consistent UTM tags, form-source records, and a sales lead log to cross-check results. Do not simply add together the revenue claimed by several advertising platforms.

## 16. Handling comments, direct messages, and sales inquiries

Social media work should not stop once content has been published. For a B2B company, someone willing to share details about a real project is usually closer to a purchasing opportunity than someone who simply clicks Like. Set up a consistent way to monitor messages and hand conversations over to sales. Customers should not have to explain their needs repeatedly. If someone only wants to know the price or specifications at first, answer what you can and gather the remaining information gradually. Do not start by sending a list of ten questions.

### 1. Do not leave the comments section unattended

The comments section is a public place where trust is built.

Reply to comments promptly.

Common types of comments include:

#### Asking about price

Do not reply only with:

> DM me.

A more helpful reply:

> Pricing depends on length, quantity, and installation type. Please send us your shelf size or project details, and we can suggest the suitable model.

#### Asking about specifications

Reply:

> We have different sizes and mounting options. Please tell us your application, such as supermarket shelf, pharmacy shelf, or display cabinet, and we can send the right specification.

#### Asking whether customization is available

Reply:

> Yes, custom length, color temperature, housing color, and mounting options are available depending on the project requirement.

### 2. A direct-message handling process

Do not start a conversation with only:

> How many pieces do you need?

A better process is:

1. Confirm the application
2. Confirm the country and project type
3. Confirm the dimensions or ask for a drawing
4. Confirm the quantity
5. Send the relevant specification sheet
6. Move the conversation to email or WhatsApp where appropriate
7. Hand the lead over to sales for follow-up

Example:

> Thanks for your interest.
> May I know what type of shelf or display fixture you are working on?
> If you can share the shelf size, application, and estimated quantity, we can suggest the suitable lighting layout and model.

### 3. Information to collect from a B2B inquiry

Where relevant, try to collect:

* Country
* Type of company
* Application
* Product type
* Dimensions
* Quantity
* Voltage requirements
* Color temperature requirements
* Installation method
* Whether customization is needed
* Project timeline
* Whether drawings are available
* Whether samples are required

## 18. A weekly operating routine and a starting plan for small teams

### Monday: Content planning

The schedule below is an example. It does not mean every post must wait until Thursday to be published. Content can be prepared and scheduled in advance after editing on Wednesday, then spread across suitable days. Comments and direct messages should still be checked daily.

Decide on the week's content:

* Two product application posts
* Two factory or production posts
* One industry knowledge post
* One short-form video designed for discovery
* One customer or project example

### Tuesday: Film content

Film:

* Product details
* Installation actions
* Factory processes
* Testing processes
* Application scenes
* Packaging and shipping

### Wednesday: Edit and design

Prepare:

* Three short videos
* Two image posts
* One carousel
* One long-form LinkedIn post
* One YouTube Short

### Thursday: Publish and engage

Publish on:

* LinkedIn
* Instagram
* Facebook
* TikTok
* YouTube Shorts

Engage by:

* Replying to comments
* Following target customers
* Participating in industry groups
* Recording potential customers

### Friday: Review performance and test advertising

Review:

* Which videos had strong retention?
* Which posts generated direct messages?
* Which creative is suitable for paid promotion?
* Which hook worked?
* Which platform performed best?

Advertising:

* Test promising creative with a small budget
* Build remarketing audiences where appropriate

### Weekend: Lightweight content and asset organization

Publish:

* Stories
* Factory moments
* Shipment updates
* Showroom details
* Short industry opinions

Organize:

* Content assets
* Topic ideas
* Customer questions
* Ideas for next week's scripts

### How a one-person team can start from zero

The weekly plan above suits a team with a reasonable amount of content and collaboration capacity. If a company has only one social media operator, it should not try to launch several regional accounts, film new videos every day, and create separate content for six platforms at the same time. Start with the two platforms closest to the real target customers. Treat the others as optional channels for repurposing content.

In Week 1, complete the website contact path, the main product information, and the social profile details. Ask the sales team for the ten questions customers ask most often. Select existing photos and videos that can answer those questions. Choose one representative product and prepare an installation video, an application image, and an FAQ-style explanation.

In Week 2, publish the content in formats suited to the two selected platforms and record customer feedback. If an installation demonstration attracts interest from target customers, use the same original footage to produce a short version and a full tutorial. There is no need to film everything again just to fill the schedule. Set aside time every day to reply to comments and direct messages.

In Week 3, check which topics attract actual potential customers. Improve the related product page and contact path. If an advertising budget is available, test one clear topic with a specific customer benefit on a small scale. There is no need to wait until the account reaches an arbitrary follower count.

In Week 4, review lead sources and quality with the sales team. Decide which topics and platforms to continue next month and whether the budget should increase. Expand into more regions, languages, or platforms only after the team can produce content consistently. Do not completely change direction just because the first month did not produce a viral post.

This four-week schedule is an example of how to organize the work, not a promise that leads will arrive within four weeks. What a small team needs most is a process it can keep following, not a huge plan that cannot be maintained.

## 19. Common social media marketing mistakes

Use this list for regular self-checks. When you find a problem, fix the issue that most affects customer understanding or inquiry handling first. You do not have to correct everything at once. For example, if a video is clear but has no contact path, add a product link or message prompt. If the company is already receiving inquiries but nobody replies promptly, fix the follow-up process before increasing the publishing frequency.

### 1. Content mistakes

* Posting only product photos
* Talking only about specifications
* Showing no application
* Addressing no customer problem
* Providing no CTA
* Ignoring the English-language audience
* Having no recurring content themes
* Publishing exactly the same content on every platform
* Putting too much text on images
* Starting videos too slowly

### 2. Asset mistakes

* Low resolution
* Shaky footage
* Cluttered backgrounds
* Poor lighting
* Distorted product appearance
* No sense of scale
* No installation context
* Showing only finished products and never the process
* Showing only the factory without explaining why it matters

### 3. Operating mistakes

* Publishing and then ignoring the post
* Not replying to comments
* Not recording customer questions
* Never reviewing data
* Never using remarketing
* Having no organized asset library
* Ignoring platform differences
* Failing to maintain the work over time

### 4. Conversion mistakes

* Replying to direct messages too slowly
* Sending a quotation before understanding the project
* Not asking about project requirements
* Not requesting drawings when they are needed
* Not sending specification sheets
* Not guiding the customer to email or WhatsApp when appropriate
* Failing to connect content activity with the sales process

## 20. Final thoughts

The real value of overseas social media marketing is not simply knowing the names of different platforms or memorizing which platform is suitable for which industry.

What matters is knowing:

1. Why people stop to view content on each platform
2. What target customers actually care about
3. How to adapt one product into content for different platforms
4. What to show in the opening seconds of a video
5. What kind of material builds trust
6. How to move from views to genuine inquiries
7. How to review data and improve the next round of work
8. How to make content support the sales process

For B2B exporters, social media is not just a place to display a brand. It is a long-term customer acquisition system.

Its ultimate goal is:

> Help target customers keep seeing you, understanding you, and trusting you—so that when a project comes up, they are willing to contact you.
