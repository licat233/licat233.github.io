01 / Where company work gets stuck

Companies rarely lack knowledge. Work gets stuck finding information, asking around and preparing it for use.

Most companies already have files, policies, product information and years of experience. The problem is that this context is scattered across systems, folders and people — and generic AI cannot use what it cannot reach.

Information is scattered

Specifications, quotations, policies and historical files live across shared drives, documents, wikis and personal folders.

AI does not know the company

Generic AI may be capable, but it does not automatically know your products, rules, history or ways of working.

Too much repetitive preparation

Employees repeatedly search, collect, reformat and prepare the same kinds of information.

Knowledge depends on people

Important experience often lives with a few long-tenured employees, so others have to keep asking.

03 / A real work pattern

When a customer asks a product question, Sales should not start by hunting for files.

Traditional workflow

common today
  1. Guess where the information might be
  2. Search specifications and shared folders
  3. Ask the colleague who knows the product
  4. Find prior quotations or similar orders
  5. Manually assemble the customer reply

With EAO

EAO
  1. Ask the Sales Assistant
  2. EAO checks company product knowledge
  3. Read approved specification files and history
  4. Prepare a reply draft with company context
  5. Sales reviews and sends
EAO is not designed to remove people from the process. It is designed to take on the searching, context gathering and repetitive preparation that slows them down.

Frequently asked questions

Questions companies usually ask before bringing AI into daily work.

EAO is designed to fit around an existing organization rather than force the organization to rebuild around AI.

About EAO

How is EAO different from simply giving employees ChatGPT?

EAO is not just another chat window. It organizes department assistants around company knowledge, files, approved tools and access boundaries so AI can work with company context rather than only general model knowledge.

Is EAO only for answering questions?

No. Depending on the tools and permissions a company enables, EAO can also help search, analyze, prepare drafts, work with files and move parts of a task forward while keeping people in the loop where needed.

Does every department use the same AI assistant?

Not necessarily. Sales, Procurement, Operations, Finance and Management can use different assistants with different knowledge, files, tools and access scopes.

Is EAO a concept project or something already used in a real company?

EAO grew out of real company operations. ARMOR is the first reference implementation, while reusable EAO components remain separate from company-private configuration and data.

Already using Feishu / existing systems?

We already have a Feishu Bot. Do we still need EAO?

A Feishu Bot can be a useful interaction channel, but EAO addresses a broader problem: how AI works with departments, company knowledge, files, tools and permissions. The two can coexist rather than replace each other.

Will EAO replace Feishu?

No. EAO is designed to work around systems a company already uses. Feishu can continue to handle communication, collaboration and business processes, while EAO adds an AI work layer around existing company assets.

Do we have to migrate our Feishu knowledge base and files into a new system?

Not necessarily. Where configured, the knowledge layer can synchronize supported Feishu knowledge and drive sources, so companies can continue maintaining information in existing systems instead of moving everything just for EAO.

Can we keep using NAS, shared drives, wikis and existing business systems?

Yes. One of EAO's core ideas is: keep your systems and add AI around them. Existing storage, knowledge sources and business tools can remain part of the company environment.

Data, permissions and deployment

Will all company data be sent to an external AI provider?

That depends on the model providers and integrations the company chooses. The EAO application can be self-hosted, but self-hosting alone does not mean every model request automatically stays local.

Can different departments be kept inside different access boundaries?

EAO is designed to support different department scopes for knowledge, files and tools. The actual boundary still depends on how the organization configures permissions and integrations.

Do we have to use a local AI model?

No. EAO can be organized around local or external model providers. A company can choose according to capability, cost, latency and data-handling requirements.

Do we have to deploy everything at once?

No. EAO is better introduced around a real business problem first. A company can start with one department or one workflow and expand only when additional capability is useful.
